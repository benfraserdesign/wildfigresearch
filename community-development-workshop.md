    <section id="about-the-research" class="border-top pt-4 mt-5">
      <h2 class="mb-3">About the research</h2>

      <p>We're conducting a piece of research, in partnership with <a href="https://localtrust.org.uk/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Local Trust</a>, to understand the current landscape of community development practice across the UK to identify what is needed to strengthen the infrastructure to support and sustain community development work in England.</p>

      <p>We're exploring:</p>
      <ul>
        <li>who is currently supporting community development across England</li>
        <li>what infrastructure exists to support community development workers, and where the gaps are</li>
        <li>where community development is taking place under different job titles</li>
        <li>what the work looks like in practice</li>
        <li>what England can learn from Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland</li>
      </ul>

      <p>The findings will help Local Trust and other key policy stakeholders understand how community development work can be better supported in the future.</p>
    </section>

    <section id="about-the-workshop" class="border-top pt-4 mt-5">
      <h2 class="mb-3">About the workshop</h2>

      <p>In this workshop we want to hear from people doing community development work (broadly defined) about what their work looks like in practice, what a 'good experience' of community development looks and feels like, and what needs to change to better support this.</p>

      <p>In particular, we want to understand:</p>
      <ul>
        <li>how people come into this work</li>
        <li>the support and training available to them</li>
        <li>the skills that underpin relational ways of working</li>
        <li>what helps or hinders their wellbeing and their ability to stay in these roles</li>
      </ul>
    </section>

    <section id="what-will-the-workshop-involve" class="border-top pt-4 mt-5">
      <h2 class="mb-3">What will the workshop involve?</h2>

      <p>This will be a safe and supportive space for you to share your experiences.</p>

      <p>The 2-hour session will include:</p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Welcome and introductions</strong> – sharing feelings about doing community development work</li>
        <li><strong>Facilitated discussion part 1</strong> – exploring your experience doing community development work, including what a 'good' experience looks and feels like to you</li>
        <li><strong>Facilitated discussion part 2</strong> – identifying what change would look like and what it would take to make this happen</li>
        <li>A comfort break</li>
      </ul>

      <p>The workshop facilitators will use a <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r0vrPSZjWMQ" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Miro board</a> to capture notes from the discussions so that we can support the sessions and develop the workshop write-up. There is no expectation of participants having previous experience of using Miro, and we will help you if you would like support.</p>

      <p>There are no right or wrong answers; we are interested in your honest views and experience. You can choose not to answer or discuss anything.</p>
    </section>

    <section id="who-should-participate" class="border-top pt-4 mt-5">
      <h2 class="mb-3">Who should participate?</h2>

      <p>We're looking for up to 10 people who do community development work in England – whether or not you describe it as 'community development' or have a community development job title – who want to share their perspectives on what works and what needs to change.</p>

      <p>By community development, we mean work that brings people together, builds on the strengths of local communities, and supports people to have a say in and take action on the issues that matter to them.</p>

      <p>This includes both <strong>paid and voluntary</strong> community development work.</p>

      <p>For example, this may include:</p>
      <ul>
        <li>people who work in a place-based role, or in a local or regional organisation that supports community development</li>
        <li>social workers, youth workers, health professionals, and outreach or engagement workers helping people build confidence, strengthen relationships or address community issues</li>
        <li>people working in regeneration, planning or economic development involving residents in shaping their neighbourhoods and local services</li>
        <li>people active in their own communities organising activities, supporting local groups, bringing neighbours together or campaigning for change, whether paid or unpaid</li>
      </ul>

      <p>This work can happen under many different job titles and across different professions. We want to hear from anyone doing this kind of work, whatever your role or title, paid or unpaid.</p>

      <p><strong>If you're unsure if this is you,</strong> please feel free to still submit an <a href="{{ page.form_url }}" target="_blank" rel="noopener">expression of interest form</a>, which asks for a short description of the work you do.</p>
    </section>
