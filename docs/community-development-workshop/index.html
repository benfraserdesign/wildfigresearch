Invitation to participate in a workshop exploring community development practice in England

We would like to invite people who are doing community development work (broadly defined, both paid and voluntary) in England to take part in an online workshop as part of our Hidden Community Development Workers research project.

The research is being delivered by Wild Fig Research, in partnership with Local Trust.

This online workshop will bring together up to 10 people to share what their work looks like in practice, what a good experience of community development work feels like, and what needs to change to better support community development work in England.

Workshop details

  • Date:Tuesday 17 November 2026
  • Time:11am – 1pm (2 hours)
  • Where:Online via Google Meet

How to get involved

Please read the information below which explains more about the workshop.

If you're interested in participating, please complete our expression of interest form by 30 October 2026.

Complete the expression of interest form

We have 10 places available. Places will be allocated to cover a range of roles, places and experiences of community development work.

We'll be in touch by 5 November to confirm places.

We'll share further information with selected participants, including a detailed workshop plan, joining instructions, and a consent form that sets out how the findings will be used and shared.

We look forward to hearing from you, and thank you for considering taking part.

About the research

We're conducting a piece of research, in partnership with Local Trust, to understand the current landscape of community development practice across the UK to identify what is needed to strengthen the infrastructure to support and sustain community development work in England.

We're exploring:

  • who is currently supporting community development across England
  • what infrastructure exists to support community development workers, and where the gaps are
  • where community development is taking place under different job titles
  • what the work looks like in practice
  • what England can learn from Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland

The findings will help Local Trust and other key policy stakeholders understand how community development work can be better supported in the future.

About the workshop

In this workshop we want to hear from people doing community development work (broadly defined) about what their work looks like in practice, what a 'good experience' of community development looks and feels like, and what needs to change to better support this.

In particular, we want to understand:

  • how people come into this work
  • the support and training available to them
  • the skills that underpin relational ways of working
  • what helps or hinders their wellbeing and their ability to stay in these roles

What will the workshop involve?

This will be a safe and supportive space for you to share your experiences.

The 2-hour session will include:

  • Welcome and introductions – sharing feelings about doing community development work
  • Facilitated discussion part 1 – exploring your experience doing community development work, including what a 'good' experience looks and feels like to you
  • Facilitated discussion part 2 – identifying what change would look like and what it would take to make this happen
  • A comfort break

The workshop facilitators will use a Miro board to capture notes from the discussions so that we can support the sessions and develop the workshop write-up. There is no expectation of participants having previous experience of using Miro, and we will help you if you would like support.

There are no right or wrong answers; we are interested in your honest views and experience. You can choose not to answer or discuss anything.

Who should participate?

We're looking for up to 10 people who do community development work in England – whether or not you describe it as 'community development' or have a community development job title – who want to share their perspectives on what works and what needs to change.

By community development, we mean work that brings people together, builds on the strengths of local communities, and supports people to have a say in and take action on the issues that matter to them.

This includes both paid and voluntary community development work.

For example, this may include:

  • people who work in a place-based role, or in a local or regional organisation that supports community development
  • social workers, youth workers, health professionals, and outreach or engagement workers helping people build confidence, strengthen relationships or address community issues
  • people working in regeneration, planning or economic development involving residents in shaping their neighbourhoods and local services
  • people active in their own communities organising activities, supporting local groups, bringing neighbours together or campaigning for change, whether paid or unpaid

This work can happen under many different job titles and across different professions. We want to hear from anyone doing this kind of work, whatever your role or title, paid or unpaid.

If you're unsure if this is you, please feel free to still submit an expression of interest form, which asks for a short description of the work you do.

Payment

As recognition for the contribution of your time and expertise, workshop participants will receive a £75 honorarium following the workshop.

This can be paid to you as an individual, or as a donation in your name to a local organisation or charity of your choice.

What this will be used for and co-authorship opportunity

Following the workshop, we'll develop and share a short, accessible summary of findings from the workshop, which you can use in your own work or share with others. All workshop participants will have the opportunity and choice to be co-authors of this output.

Workshop findings will also be written up and incorporated into our final report for Local Trust, and used to inform an ecosystem map we're developing to accompany this.

We'll share all of the project's outputs with you.

You can choose whether you wish to be credited or anonymous, and we'll send more information about this to selected participants.

Our commitment to inclusion and accessibility

We're committed to being people-centred in our approach and to recognising barriers to participation.

As a result, this workshop is:

  • Scheduled to avoid caring and childcare responsibilities (late morning)
  • Fully online to reduce travel barriers
  • Focused on providing digital support if you need help joining online or feeling confident in the workshop space
  • Designed to respect lived and learnt experience and to avoid extraction or tokenism

If you have any accessibility needs or requirements, please let us know so we can support your participation.
